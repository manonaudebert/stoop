Design System — Porcelain v0.1 · May 2026

An opinionated
civic data product.

stoop helps prospective and current tenants navigate NYC building complaints with clarity, comparison, and a point of view.

White surfaces. Ink type. Oxblood as the only color of consequence. Numbers are always comparative — every figure shows its neighborhood median, every claim cites its receipt. The interface should feel like a publication, not a dashboard.

01 · Color

Three groups, one accent

Surfaces, ink, and data. Oxblood is the only chromatic UI color — used for selection, brand, and primary editorial moments. Data colors live on the map and in charts; they never appear in chrome.

Rule. Outlier markers and outlier copy are always ink/primary, never red. Red is reserved for "loud" buildings in aggregate, the selected state, and primary editorial links. Black is louder than red here — a deliberate inversion of the dashboard cliché.

03 · Layout

Spacing & radius

A six-step spacing scale on a 4px base. Three radii. Borders are 0.5px hairlines that lift on hover. No drop shadows anywhere — depth comes from typography and density, not Z-axis tricks.

Borders

All borders are 0.5px solid #E5E5E5. Hover lifts to #A3A3A3. Selected state is #7F1D1D oxblood. No shadows, ever.

05 · Voice

Editorial voice

Three rules: make a claim (don't hedge), cite the receipt (always comparative), and name the texture (what kind of complaints, what's the trend). Headlines do interpretive work. Subtext provides the data. Hedging language signals a dashboard; decisive language signals a publication.

Do
"Among the loudest buildings on the Upper East Side — driven mostly by permit issues, though activity has cooled since 2023."
Specific. Comparative. Names the cause. Acknowledges the nuance.
Don't
"This building has significant complaint activity compared to its neighborhood — review the details below."
Vague. Hedge-y. Defers to the user instead of making a claim.
Headline pattern. [Comparative claim] — [primary cause], though [nuance]. If you can't fill in the second clause from the data, the headline isn't ready.
